Contact release and reorientation in the real_v1 fingertip models

In a 400 ms continuation of the D1 screwdriver grasp, numerical control derivatives improved the same sampled 1 mm packed-pad plan from 0.086 to 0.156 mean orientation gain with 8/8 perturbed grasps held. A sampled index release recontacted in 6/8 starts but gained 0.109 when held and failed on the convex mesh. Plain DiffMJX produced a local derivative across recontact on 2 mm pads; exact 1 mm MJX rollout compilation remained unfinished when the run stopped at 7 min 55 s.

D1 hardware-derived hand · 0.8 s supported grasp, then 0.4 s unsupported continuation · 1 mm packed pads, convex mesh pad, and single-sphere control · 1 ms integration step

Terms and metrics

Vertical cosine is the cosine between the screwdriver axis and world vertical; 1 is upright. Orientation gain is final minus initial vertical cosine. Held means the tool stayed within 8 mm of its starting height and each fingertip supplied at least 0.5 N normal force at 400 ms. Release duration counts 10 ms samples at which the index fingertip supplied less than 0.05 N. Recontact means at least 20 ms of release followed by at least 0.5 N index force at 400 ms.

Cross-entropy method (CEM) repeatedly samples control plans and shifts a Gaussian distribution toward high-scoring plans. Local gradient search uses L-BFGS-B, an optimizer with control limits, and two-point numerical control derivatives in central processing unit (CPU) MuJoCo. Hybrid uses half of its 256 forward evaluations for CEM and half for local refinement. MuJoCo XLA (MJX) is the JAX simulation backend; MuJoCo-Warp is the graphics processing unit (GPU) backend used by the current mjlab trainer. Automatic differentiation (AD) computes derivatives through MJX. A finite difference (FD) estimates a derivative from positive and negative control perturbations. The 400 ms searches used numerical derivatives, while the prior 20 ms study measured AD on the released DiffMJX fork. MCP denotes the metacarpophalangeal finger joint.

Control derivative through index recontact

The sampled 2 mm pad plan reached a positive index gap of 38.4 µm at 280 ms. CPU index normal force rose from 0.000 to 0.571 N during the next 10 ms. The released fork used its ordinary contact derivative with contacts from distance disabled. Its AD derivative compiled in 70.3 s and took 9.84 s warm. The CPU finite differences were stable at 0.01, 0.003 and 0.001 rad perturbations.

Grip perturbations and simulator transfer

Eight deterministic screwdriver pose jitters were replayed from the 0.8 s grasp. MuJoCo 3.3.1, MuJoCo 3.6 and MuJoCo-Warp started from their corresponding CPU held states. The Warp test changes the simulation backend while retaining the MuJoCo contact model. Plans were fixed after seed-1 search.

The free hybrid gained 0.070 vertical cosine over its own 128-sample seed while retaining 8 of eight packed-pad grasps; convex-mesh retention fell from 8 to 4. The release hybrid kept the same 6 of eight packed-pad grasps as its sampled seed and added 0.001 held gain. MuJoCo 3.3.1 and Warp reproduced these packed-pad held counts; failed rollouts sometimes followed different orientation paths.

Simulation cost and contact fidelity

Native MJX 3.6 and the released DiffMJX fork ran on the same RTX 4070 Ti SUPER. Each 20 ms GPU test used one world and 64-bit arithmetic except the row labeled 32-bit. The 2 mm packed surface has 681 spheres; the task surface has 2,388 spheres. The fork's exact 1 mm forward compilation completed, but its 20 ms rollout compilation remained unfinished when the run stopped at 7.92 min and 11.49 GiB peak resident memory. The 2 mm packed MJX scene had a maximum initial fingertip-gap difference of 1.04 µm from CPU MuJoCo. The corresponding single-sphere error was 0.30 mm. The mesh/capsule comparator disagreed by 13–22 mm at thumb and index; its rollout has a different contact geometry from the mesh-pad CPU scene. The prior exact 1 mm single-step study measured near-micrometre packed contact parity.

Implications for differentiable contact

Numerical local derivatives improved the same sampled packed-pad plan from 0.086 to 0.156 mean orientation gain across 8 held grip jitters. Its mesh replay retained 8 grasps before refinement and 4 after. Direct local search reached 0.163 gain on its 6 held jitters. From a fully opened index, local search failed to discover a retained grasp; sampling found recontact. Refinement of that sampled release plan kept 6 of eight grasps and changed held gain from 0.107 to 0.109. The packed release hybrid held 0 of eight mesh replays. The 400 ms result supports local refinement within a favorable contact regime and shows no stronger behavior from deliberate release in this task slice. Plain AD tracked the physical recontact derivative; the contacts-from-distance backward rule changed that derivative by severalfold at the measured onset.

The released DiffMJX study also found gradient control effective in two in-hand tasks, while contacts from distance had little effect there because gravity supplied contact. Its appendix reports multi-hour gradient planning in a MyoHand task and a 35 min 1,024-sample baseline. The reported contact-from-distance forces can oppose grasp acquisition when several fingertips approach from different sides. Our 20 ms contact-gradient study measured the fork's AD on the exact packed surface; this 400 ms search measures whether local control derivatives help select a manipulation plan.

Methods and reproducibility

The D1 scene is generated from assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml and the deployed D1 grasp plan. The palm display plate and support post are disabled during continuation. The packed-pad test masks pad-to-pad collisions while retaining sphere-to-tool contacts. The source is scripts/real_v1_contact_gait_search.py; cross-model CPU replay is scripts/real_v1_contact_gait_transfer.py; Warp replay is scripts/real_v1_contact_gait_warp.py. Full commands, software versions, raw JSON and the stopped MJX job are listed in docs/experiments/20261007-contact-gait/README.md.

MuJoCo's MJX documentation describes automatic differentiation in MJX-JAX and its contact-graph performance limits. MuJoCo Playground offers policy-gradient training on MJX-JAX and MJX-Warp. Its ordinary policy-gradient run estimates policy updates from sampled trajectories.
