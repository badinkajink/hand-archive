Contact gradients in the real_v1 screwdriver grasp

Native MJX 3.6 and the released fork completed one step of the 2,388-pad grasp with at most 1.84 µm initial tool-gap difference from CPU MuJoCo. Over 20 ms, the fork gradient selected a 0.1 rad control action that scored 0.111245 in CPU MuJoCo; CPU finite differences scored 0.111245 and the best of 128 samples scored 0.111241. The warm three-control gradient took 35.7 s versus 23.5 ms for six CPU finite-difference evaluations. Contact-from-distance with the scan-loop solver scored 0.111239 at 56.7 s.

Terms and metrics

MuJoCo XLA (MJX) is MuJoCo’s JAX simulation backend. Central processing unit (CPU) identifies the processor used for every measured JAX run. Jacobian-vector product (JVP) is one directional automatic derivative of a simulator output.

Contact gap is the signed minimum distance between a fingertip sphere and the tool surface, in millimetres; a negative value is penetration and zero is first touch. Forward error is the largest absolute difference in generalized position or velocity after the same command from the same initial state. The position maximum mixes metres, radians and quaternion components. The velocity maximum mixes metres per second and radians per second. The position column multiplies its raw numerical value by 106; it is a parity check, not a physical distance.

Control derivative is the change in a final tool quantity per radian of finger-yaw servo target bias. Automatic differentiation (AD) differentiates JAX code. Central finite difference (FD) evaluates positive and negative yaw-target perturbations; the step size appears beside each result. Vertical cosine is the cosine between the tool axis and world vertical; 1 is upright. Contact torque is the tool constraint torque around world x, in newton-metres, at the end of the stated rollout. AD error is the absolute difference between AD and CPU FD divided by the magnitude of CPU FD for the indicated yaw target. Direction cosine is the dot product of two unit-length three-yaw gradient directions; 1 means identical directions. Action gain is final vertical cosine minus the unchanged-yaw result after 20 ms.

Software and earlier experiments

Our April diffmjx-mvp used ordinary MJX with a sigmoid distance reward on a cube. It did not run the authors’ DiffMJX code. The released DiffMJX project supplies an MJX fork with contacts from distance, a scan-based solver loop and smooth collision options, plus adaptive integration. This study ran the fork’s first two switches. Sphere-to-capsule collision already has a smooth collision routine; the soft collision option was off.

MuJoCo Playground can use MJX-JAX or MJX-Warp. Its reinforcement-learning examples use trajectory samples for policy-gradient training. Simulator derivatives enter through a trajectory-optimization or differentiable-learning algorithm. Our current reorientation trainer uses MuJoCo-Warp through mjlab.

Twenty-millisecond control response

The plain fork returned finite derivatives for all three yaw targets after 20 ms. Its 0.1 rad action raised CPU vertical cosine from 0.111039 to 0.111245. The CPU finite-difference action reached 0.111245; the contact-from-distance action reached 0.111239. The plain and contact-from-distance three-direction gradients took 35.7 and 56.7 s when warm, after 83.1 and 123 s compilation.

On the 1 mm packed surface, contact-from-distance and the scan-loop solver changed the 20 ms index-yaw vertical-cosine derivative from 0.0019291 to 0.0016262 per radian. The three-control action direction moved from cosine 0.9984 to 0.98 against CPU finite differences. The single-sphere capsule control gave a -5.13% change in the same index-yaw derivative.

The 20 ms fork index-yaw finite difference for vertical cosine was 0.0021183 per radian at 0.01 rad and 0.0019306 at 0.0003 rad; the automatic derivative was 0.0019291. CPU finite differences stayed between 0.0018586 and 0.0018605 over the sweep. The 0.01 rad fork finite difference samples a wider response than its local derivative.

Implications for the task suite

The released fork produced finite force and motion derivatives on the packed held grasp. Its plain gradient selected a turn action with the same gain as CPU finite differences at substantially higher CPU cost. Contact-from-distance with the scan-loop solver changed the gradient and slightly reduced this action's 20 ms CPU score. First-touch optimization is the remaining test of that contact-gradient modification.

Methods and reproducibility

The D1 design and plan come from the real_v1 hardware family; the starting tool pose has seed 1 jitter. The palm is fixed, the grip lasts 0.8 s, and each continuation uses 1 ms implicit-fast MuJoCo steps, an elliptic friction cone and Newton contact solver with impratio 100. The CPU pad-mask check used MuJoCo 3.6.0; the official fork used MuJoCo 3.3.1 and JAX 0.11.2. The fork packages were cloned at pinned commits in the runbook. All JAX runs used CPU, so GPU throughput is unmeasured. The released 1 mm forward process peaked at 5.8 GiB resident memory; the one-direction derivative peaked at 7.21 GiB. The native 1 mm forward process peaked at 8.13 GiB and wrote its one-step output before the resource guard stopped the process. The plain and contact-from-distance 20 ms three-control gradient processes peaked at 7.44 and 10.3 GiB.
