Friction-row time constant, contact reduction and edge contact in Newton’s hydroelastic fingertip model

Newton’s friction-row time constant sets its creep and leaves its spin-onset torque unchanged: from 2 to 20 ms the twist creep rises tenfold while the onset arm stays within 2 %, and the braked swing stops within 2.0° of Drake’s at 10–40 ms instead of swinging to 130° at the default 3.9 ms. Its pressure field gives the law’s friction arm (0.973 against 0.974 mm at 1 N over all faces); the contact reduction cuts it to 0.801 mm, which is the 12–23 % torque deficit. On a square edge Drake’s field matches the law within 3 %; the 1 mm pads, whose spheres press along the line from the edge, carry 2.0× the law’s force at 0.5 N and 1.04× at 3 N; Newton’s voxel field rounds the edge off and carries 0.32× at 0.5 N with 0.5 mm voxels and 0.83× with 0.25 mm.

Terms and metrics

pad,
One fingertip’s contact with the tool; two pads pinch it, and is the normal force each rail applies, in N.
pressure law
The hydroelastic field of a compliant sphere of radius = 10.55 mm and modulus = 10 MPa, the distance from its centre, integrated over the part of the tool surface inside the sphere with no simulator (scripts/hydroelastic_arm_integral.py). On the screwdriver it gives the friction arms 0.820, 0.974 and 1.281 mm at 0.5, 1 and 3 N.
onset arm, sliding arm
Task 2 ramps a torque about the pinch axis; the torque at spin onset divided by is the onset arm, in mm per pad. The sliding arm is the torque during the first 60 ms of spin, corrected for the tool’s angular acceleration, divided by . /law: over the pressure law’s arm.
arm ratio
Onset arm at 3 N over onset arm at 0.5 N. The pressure law gives 1.565 on the screwdriver, a Hertz contact 1.817.
pre-onset rotation
Rotation of the tool between the start of the torque ramp and spin onset, in degrees.
creep
Steady sliding under a load held at half the onset value for 1 s: the tool’s axial speed in task 1 (µm/s) and its turning speed in task 2 (°/s). Rigid Coulomb friction gives zero.
largest swing, 80° force
Task 5 lowers the pinch from 6 to 0.2 N over 4 s with the tool’s centre of mass 15 mm off the pinch line. Largest swing: the largest angle of the tool from horizontal (90° hanging; Drake 87.3°). 80° force: the pinch force when the swing first passes 80°, in N; a swing braked by friction passes 80° late, at a low force (Drake 0.39 N).
,
Newton’s friction gain (N s/m) becomes, in SolverMuJoCo, the time constant of each contact’s friction rows (solreffriction), (1). A shorter makes friction stick harder; the MuJoCo pads use their contact time constant, 10 ms.
Newton’s hydroelastic stiffness divided by the pad–tool effective mass of the solver’s MuJoCo model, 17.4 g, so that MuJoCo’s acceleration-space contact realises the pressure law (bed page, Newton paragraph).
contact reduction
Newton merges the faces of a hydroelastic contact surface into a few representative contacts (8–10 per pad here) before the solver; all faces passes every face (70–350 per pad).
voxel size
Grid spacing of the signed-distance fields from which Newton builds each shape’s pressure field; 0.5 mm unless stated.
approach
Distance the pad centre has moved toward the tool past first touch, in mm.
force at the law’s approach
The pad placed at the approach at which the pressure law carries , and the force along the pinch axis that each model’s contact law gives there, with no solver dynamics and no friction; over . The law gives 1.
patch half-length
Largest distance along the edge (world ) of a contact carrying over 1 % of the largest, in mm.
relaxation time
Drake’s dissipation time for hydroelastic contact, 10 ms on the bed.

Friction-row time constant

The friction-row time constant sets how far Newton’s tip creeps and leaves the spin-onset torque where it was. From = 2 to 20 ms the onset arm at 1 N stays at 0.749–0.766 mm (0.77–0.79 of the law) and the arm ratio at 1.647–1.660, while the task 2 creep at 1 N rises from 0.16 to 1.72 °/s and the task 1 creep from 3.6 to 36.0 µm/s, in proportion to (Table 1). At = 10 ms, the pads’ value, Newton creeps 0.85 °/s against the pads’ 1.21 and Drake’s 0.008.

In task 5 the tool swings past hanging at = 1, 3, 3.9, 5, 7 ms (largest swing 102–138°) and stops within 3° of Drake’s 87.3° at 2, 10, 15, 20, 40 ms, so the overshoot past hanging is set by the friction rows. The braking is not: at 10–20 ms Newton passes 80° at 1.24–1.28 N, in the first fall, where Drake and the pads hold the tool partway and pass 80° at 0.39 and 0.40 N, 1.4 s later (Figure 1). At 40 ms the 80° force falls to 0.64 N. Measured at 3 s, after the first fall, the tool hangs at 78.6° in Drake and 76.2° with the pads; Newton’s first fall ends at 88.0, 86.0, 82.4, 79.5° for = 10, 15, 20 and 40 ms, so a longer friction-row time constant catches the tool earlier.

A shorter arm alone does not make the tool fall through. MuJoCo condim 4 with its torsion schedule scaled to 0.75–0.90 of the law, the range of Newton’s reduced arm, starts the swing earlier (10° at 1.58–1.87 N against 1.43 unscaled) but still holds the tool partway and passes 80° at 0.45–0.52 N (brake_torque_scale.jsonl). Newton’s tool loses its friction torque during the fall by some other mechanism.

Contact reduction and voxel size

Newton’s pressure field gives the law’s arm; its contact reduction loses 9–18 % of it. Over all faces of the contact surface the static arm is 0.813 and 0.973 mm at 0.5 and 1 N against the law’s 0.819 and 0.974; the eight reduced contacts give 0.714 and 0.801 mm, and 1.159 mm at 3 N against 1.279 (Table 2, Figure 2). In task 2 the onset arm with all faces is 1.04 and 1.03 of the law at 0.5 and 1 N, against 0.83 and 0.77 reduced; Drake gives 1.02 and 1.01. The torque deficit and the arm ratio of 1.656 therefore come from the reduction, which keeps few contacts near the rim of the patch, where the lever is longest.

With all faces at 3 N the pad sinks 0.475 mm against the law’s 0.363, the contact surface grows to 348 faces per pad of which the solver loads 235, and its arm, 1.795 mm, is that of the deeper patch. A step costs 5.1 ms against 2.9 reduced; in task 2 at 3 N the tool starts to spin at 0.19 of the law’s torque, and in task 5 the tool falls at 5.4 N, as soon as the pinch starts to drop from 6 N. Without reduction Newton’s hydroelastic tip holds the law up to 1 N and fails above it.

Finer or coarser voxels keep the reduced arm short: at 0.25 and 1 mm the onset arm is 0.77–0.88 of the law over 0.5–3 N, and the static arm at 0.5 N is 0.643 and 0.684 mm against 0.714 at 0.5 mm. The voxel size moves which faces the reduction keeps, not the pressure field’s arm.

Contact on a square edge

The bar is 20 mm square, 100 mm long and 24.5 g, turned 45° about its axis so that each pad presses on an edge between two faces inclined 45° to the pinch axis (hom_contact_rig spec field bar20). At 0.5, 1 and 3 N the law gives an approach of 0.414, 0.547 and 0.854 mm (the cylinder: 0.148, 0.209, 0.363), a patch 6.7 mm long at 1 N, a peak pressure of 0.52 MPa at the edge and sliding arms of 0.958, 1.111 and 1.410 mm (ratio 1.472).

Drake’s field, cut exactly by the bar’s faces, gives 0.97–0.99 of the law’s force at the law’s approach and its arm within 1 %. The 1 mm pads give 2.02, 1.55 and 1.04 of it at 0.5, 1 and 3 N: a pad sphere of radius 0.75 mm touches the edge over ±0.72 mm across it at 1 N and pushes along the line from the edge to its centre, so each sphere near the edge carries its full share of pad area where the law loads a strip 0.77 mm wide on each face. The excess falls as the edge sinks past the sphere radius and the faces carry the load (Table 3, Figure 3).

Newton errs the other way. Its signed-distance fields interpolate the bar’s corner between grid points and round it off, so the edge reaches less deep into the pad than the geometry says: over all faces Newton gives 0.04–0.46 of the law’s force at 1 mm voxels, 0.32–0.78 at 0.5 mm and 0.83–0.93 at 0.25 mm, from 0.5 to 3 N. The reduced contacts give the same force as all faces (0.32–0.80), so on the edge the shortfall is the voxel size.

Held at N with μ = 1, the pads come to rest short of the law’s approach: Drake at 0.421 mm and the pads at 0.404 mm for 1 N against the law’s 0.547. The faces meet the pinch axis at 45°, the friction angle of μ = 1, so friction along the faces can hold the pad wherever it stops: in Drake the pressure carries 51 % of the pinch force at 0.5–3 N and friction the rest. The settled approach and the patch it gives then depend on how each model’s friction lets the pad creep in, which is why Table 3 compares the contact laws at a set approach.

In task 2 the pads start to spin at 0.81–1.00 of the law’s arm and Drake at 0.88–1.05 (Table 4); on the screwdriver both were within 7 %. Newton’s rounded edge lets the pad sink deeper than the law’s approach under the pinch (0.577 against 0.547 mm at 1 N, 1.005 against 0.854 at 3 N), the patch widens, and its onset arm is 1.20–1.60 of the law. Drake turns the bar 10.4° before onset at 1 N, against 0.02° on the screwdriver. Its relaxation time changes this: at 1 ms the bar still turns 8.4° and the onset arm is 0.88 of the law; at 0.1 s it turns 25.8° and the onset arm is 1.85, while on the screwdriver 0.1 s gives an onset arm of 0.991 mm against 0.987 at 10 ms. A twist about the pinch axis moves each point of an inclined face along the face normal, and Drake’s dissipation resists that normal motion with a torque proportional to the spin speed.

What this does not settle

  • Braking of the swing in Newton. With ≥ 10 ms Newton no longer overshoots but falls through 80° at 1.2–1.3 N where Drake passes it at 0.39 N, and a 10–25 % shorter arm does not reproduce that in condim 4. Log the torque about the pinch axis of Newton’s solver contact forces during task 5 (NewtonRig.mj_contacts every step) against at the same pinch force; the fall happens with gravity on and the weight loading the friction cone, so repeat task 2 at 1.3 N with gravity on: a torque that collapses only then puts the fall in the combined translational and torsional friction of the eight reduced contacts.
  • All faces at 3 N. Newton’s unreduced contact sinks 31 % deeper than the law at 3 N with a third of its faces unloaded. Rerun contact_bed_newton.py static --models newton_hydro_unreduced_mc --N 3 with 500 solver iterations and a sparse Jacobian; an approach that returns to 0.363 mm puts the shortfall in the solve.
  • Voxel size on the hand. On the edge Newton needs 0.25 mm voxels to come within 17 % of the law’s force. The printed fingertip’s 2.7 mm fillets and the screwdriver are curved, not sharp; set SDF_VOXEL in scripts/newton_turn.py to 0.25 mm and rerun the 40 placements of the three-finger turn: a turn that changes by more than its 0.5 mm run-to-run spread puts the hand’s Newton result in the voxel size.
  • Pad resolution on an edge. The 1 mm pads carry 2× the law’s force on the edge at 0.5 N. Run contact_bed_edge.py kinematic with a 0.5 mm pad (spec mj:spheres:s0.5:rs0.375:ir100:tr0.03:bar20): the excess should scale with the sphere radius if it is the rounding of the edge.
  • Drake’s dissipation on inclined faces. Repeat task 2 on the bar in Drake at = 1, 3, 10, 30 and 100 ms; an onset arm that tends to the law as falls makes Drake’s bar result a dissipation setting.
