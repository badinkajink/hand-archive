Presliding compliance, hysteresis and Hertz load exponents for sphere-pad fingertips with native MuJoCo elements

No model on the bed stores tangential displacement: under a force cycled at half the slip force the 1 mm pads and Drake creep and recover nothing, where Mindlin’s elastic contact moves 40 µm at 1 N and recovers 38. Three MuJoCo-native additions supply what is missing, one property each: a spring-mounted skin gives 37 µm of recoverable presliding with no hysteresis at 1.3× the pads’ GPU cost; spring-loaded ball joints under the spheres (rolling bristles) add partial slip and a 6.5 µJ loop against Mindlin’s 6.1, at 27× the cost; and an impedance that softens with depth gives Hertz’s load exponents (0.70 and 0.35 against 2/3 and 1/3) at no cost.

Terms and metrics

presliding displacement
Task 8 cycles an axial force on the pinched tool between ±, , half the slip force : 0 → in 0.5 s, → in 1 s, → in 1 s, → 0 in 0.5 s. Presliding displacement: the tool’s axial displacement at the first peak, in µm.
recovered displacement
The part of the presliding displacement that returns when the force first comes back to zero (t = 1.0 s), in µm. An elastic contact recovers most of it; a creeping contact keeps sliding while the force is positive and recovers nothing (negative values).
loop area
Work done by the force around the full cycle → → , , in µJ: the energy the contact dissipates per cycle. Mindlin’s partial slip gives a closed loop; creep gives a loop proportional to .
initial tangential stiffness
Slope of force against displacement over the first 5–20 % of on the first loading, both pads in parallel, in kN/m.
approach, RMS patch radius
Task 9 holds the pinch at N = 0.25–4 N. Approach: the pad centre’s travel past first touch. RMS patch radius: force-weighted RMS distance of the −x pad’s contacts from their centroid, in the plane normal to the pinch axis. Their log–log slopes against N are the load exponents.

largest swing, 80° force

At the same 10 MPa the pressure law spreads the load over a patch about twice Hertz’s radius (RMS 1.06 against 0.49 mm at 1 N) and carries twice the torque; Hertz matches the law’s patch at about 0.8 MPa. Point contact, sphere pads and Drake on the new tasks

Point contact, the 1 mm pads and Drake have no tangential elasticity. Under the T8 cycle their tool creeps at a speed set by the friction rows (Drake’s regularised friction 100× slower), keeps creeping while the force is positive, and recovers nothing; the loop they trace is creep (Table 2). Their T9 load exponents are the pressure law’s: the patch grows with the overlap, as in approach and in radius, with no load outside the overlap.

Candidates

(a) Compliant skin

The pad spheres sit on a child body joined to the fingertip by two slides tangent to the pad and a hinge about its normal, with springs at Mindlin’s and Lubkin’s initial stiffness at 1 N (13.8 kN/m, 8.55 mN m/rad), critical damping, and friction made stiff with impratio 1000. The skin is 2 g with 18 g of armature on its slides: MuJoCo softens a contact’s friction rows in proportion to the contact’s inverse weight, and with the bare 2 g skin the tool slid 49 µm over the spheres during each force ramp. Damping at the pads’ 20 ms relaxation (8× critical) made the tool lead and trail the skin with the force rate and reversed the loop.

T8: presliding 18.8, 37.4 and 111.2 µm at 0.5, 1 and 3 N (Mindlin 25.4, 40.3, 83.8), recovered 36.3 µm at 1 N (38.0), loop 0.29, 0.29 and −4.67 µJ (1.93, 6.13, 38.3). T9 exponents 0.501 and 0.248. T2 onset arm 1.03, 1.06 and 0.07 of the pressure law, 7.5° before onset at 1 N. At 3 N the torque ramp winds the hinge at 53°/s, above the task’s 30°/s spin detection, so that onset is not a slip measurement. T1 effective 1.007. T5 largest swing 87.3°, 80° at 0.392 N. Cost: 15 µs per step on one core at 1 N, 0.40 µs per world-step in a 4,096-world MuJoCo-Warp batch.

(e) Rolling bristles

Each sphere within 20° of the pad’s pole sits on its own body with a ball joint at its centre and a rotational spring , with = 2.7 mm so that the ~11 spheres in contact at 1 N give Mindlin’s stiffness: the contact point rolls elastically before that sphere’s Coulomb contact slips, a brush with one bristle per sphere. With 1 mg spheres and no armature the bristles go unstable under tangential load at 1 and 2 ms; armature worth 0.2 g at the contact still does; 2 g at each contact runs.

T8: presliding 25.3, 33.0 and 49.4 µm at 0.5, 1 and 3 N (Mindlin 25.4, 40.3, 83.8), recovered 29.7 µm at 1 N (38.0), loop 3.55, 6.47 and 33.68 µJ (1.93, 6.13, 38.3). T9 exponents 0.505 and 0.250. T2 onset arm 0.95, 0.96 and 0.89 of the pressure law, 5.7° before onset at 1 N. T1 effective 0.998. T5 largest swing 87.1°, 80° at 0.397 N. Cost: 86 µs per step on one core at 1 N, 8.13 µs per world-step in a 4,096-world MuJoCo-Warp batch.

What this does not settle

  • Flex and lattice candidates. MuJoCo-Warp 3.14 has flex elasticity and flex-vertex contact with spheres, boxes, cylinders and meshes (no flex–SDF, no quadratic flex), so (b) is buildable: a flexcomp shell over the tip cap, pinned at the back, with the stated Young’s modulus and Poisson ratio. (c), sphere nodes on normal slides with anchor and neighbour springs (CSLC), is not built. Both answer T9’s lateral spread, which no candidate here changes.
  • Cheaper bristles. The bristles cost 26× the pads batched and need 2 g of armature per contact to run at 1 ms. Two hinges per sphere in place of a ball joint, and a soft weld whose impedance divides out the rotational inverse inertia as the pads do for the normal direction, would remove a third of the dofs and the armature; rerun T8 and the cost.
  • The modulus. Every candidate is sized on Hertz at 10 MPa, where the pressure law’s patch is twice Hertz’s radius. An indentation test on the printed tip (force against depth) gives and the exponent; a slow tangential cycle on it measures presliding and loop area for T8 directly.
