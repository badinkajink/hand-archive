MorphoHand · UHAS integration · branch experiment/uhas

Can a frozen cross-embodiment policy score our hands?

We put four MorphoHand designs into UHAS's in-hand cube-repose task under the released multi-hand policy, to get a morphology evaluator with no training-seed draw. The policy runs on all four. None of them reposes the cube.

2026-08-02 4 hands + LEAP reference ≈14 000 goal attempts All_grippers/model_14999.pt

The result, first

Every MorphoHand design scores 0–1 % on UHAS's repose task; LEAP, which the policy was trained on, scores 51.7 %. All four of our hands' 95 % confidence intervals sit inside [0 %, 3.4 %] and overlap each other, so the evaluator currently cannot rank our designs — which is the one thing we wanted it for.

Two real bugs in UHAS's calibration were found and fixed along the way, and the cube-size hypothesis is now closed twice over. Neither was the blocker. What the work did produce is a failure taxonomy: "0 %" was concealing a hand that cannot hold the cube and a hand that holds it better than anything we have built and simply never turns it. Those need opposite fixes, and only the renders and a motion probe could tell them apart.

And the offline design metric does not survive contact with an outcome. Six cube-controlled designs spanning a 1.6× range of authority were built and run: it orders their drop rate almost perfectly, at a Spearman of −0.94, and has no relationship with goal approach, at −0.03. It is a grip-retention metric that was being read as a manipulation metric. Separately, an 82-minute finetune of the frozen policy on our best hand moved the repose rate 0.00 % → 0.00 %, so the palm-bracing counter-argument is still open and still unsupported. Both in E8.

Why bother with someone else's cube task

Every evaluator the morphology programme has used so far has been our own trained policy, and that is where the design search stalled: the per-design policy draw dominates the design signal (per-draw reorient sd 0.3–0.5, invisible to the health gate), so a search over that evaluator mostly measures seeds. H06_04 is the programme's one confirmed win and it cost n=4 replications to establish.

A frozen, pretrained, cross-embodiment policy is a different kind of evaluator, and its appeal is precisely that it removes the draw: identical weights drive every hand, so a difference between two designs cannot be a training-seed difference. That — not the cube task itself — is the reason to care. The question was never "can our hand do UHAS's task"; it was "does UHAS give us a zero-variance morphology evaluator, and what does it cost to make it valid?"

How the action space works — the five command slots

Worth stating plainly, because everything below depends on it and UHAS's README does not say it. The policy emits 15 numbers, always, for every hand: (1 + len(vector_phis)) · max_fingers = 3 × 5. A slot is not a finger — it is a canonical driving plane on the sphere, a half-plane through the polar axis at one azimuth, numbered 0–4 the way a five-fingered hand's fingers would sit (2 = the middle of the hand, 4 = the opposing thumb). Each slot's three numbers are one lateral command (where around the sphere that finger sits) and two radius offsets, at fixed polar angles φ = 50° and 120°, which define a radial profile r(φ) — the sphere's deformation in that plane, in sphere radii, range ±2.

CIK then curls that finger's joints until its links lie against the deformed profile. The policy never speaks in joints; it says "bulge the object-shaped surface here, shrink it there", and each hand's own CIK translates. Fingers are assigned to slots automatically and geometrically: each finger root's azimuth in the sphere frame is measured, the circular-median finger takes slot 2, and the rest fan out by sorted azimuth. perp comes out [[4], [2], [0, 1]] — thumb, index, and a middle finger claiming two slots.

What that costs a 3-finger hand. Slot 3 goes unclaimed, so its 3 dimensions are inert. Of the middle finger's two slots, flexion is read from the first (slot 0) while both slots' lateral targets write to the same joint index, so the later write wins — slot 1's lateral is used, slot 0's is discarded along with slot 1's two radius dimensions. 6 of 15 action dimensions drive nothing on perp. Structural, not a bug.

And why we touched the slots at all. The lateral clamp is per slot, and utils_urdf.py:2364 special-cases slot 4 to a hard-coded thumb azimuth — when that is wrong the band can exclude zero and pin a finger's lateral joint for the whole episode. That is a real bug and it is fixed (E3). But the fix moved no outcome, every interval overlapping, so slot work is a correctness chore rather than a lever. The lever is E6.

E0–E1

What was blocking, and why none of it was the hand

Three things stood between an exported MorphoHand and a number. All three turned out to be properties of the tooling, not of our morphology.

The reference number nearly came out wrong. The first LEAP run reported 0.0 % success and 85 % drop, which reads as a broken integration. It was the evaluator: _get_rewards sets reset_goal_buf, then calls _reset_target_pose, whose last line zeroes it — and successes is zeroed by _reset_idx. Both of the env's success signals are cleared inside step(), after the check that sets them, so anything reading them afterwards sees zeros and scores a working policy as total failure. The tell was in the data: best-alignment bottomed out at 0.1003 rad against a 0.1000 tolerance. A distribution whose floor sits exactly on the threshold it is compared against is an instrument problem, not a policy problem.

URDF→USD needs no GUI. UHAS's add_hand.md prescribes an Isaac Sim GUI import; UrdfConverter is the same importer driven headlessly.

UHAS's constructor assumes fingers claim all five canonical slots. LEAP's four fingers claim five; our three claim four, and the slot table is then indexed out of range — which reads as "3-finger hands unsupported" but is an unstated assumption. An unclaimed slot just means no finger drives that plane. Patched to index by slot with a live_slots mask; the shipped hands are byte-identical.

E4

What we measured

One protocol for every hand: 64 environments, 3000 control steps, the released All_grippers checkpoint, deterministic policy. An attempt is one goal orientation, and it ends in a repose, a drop, or a 30-second timeout.

E2 · E5

The cube is not the problem — closed twice

This was the leading hypothesis going in, and it has a clean answer. UHAS sizes the cube to the hand: cube_scale = (7/6) · sphere_radius / 0.09119, so the ratio of cube diagonal to sphere diameter is a constant 0.66479 for every hand — LEAP gets a 7.00 cm cube, H06_04 gets 5.45 cm, automatically. "Too big for our smaller hand" is not an available explanation.

That leaves the sharper question: is UHAS's ratio, calibrated on 4- and 5-finger hands, right for three fingers, which leave wider gaps on the sphere? So we swept it — on perp, the hand that holds the cube and won't turn it, with LEAP as the control.

E3

Two real calibration bugs — fixed, and not the blocker

min_offsets/max_offsets in sphere_cik.json are the runtime lateral command range: the env maps a policy action onto that interval and clamps into it. If the interval excludes zero, the neutral action is unreachable and the finger's lateral joint is pinned at a large fixed angle for the whole episode. Ours excluded zero, for two independent reasons.

One: utils_urdf.py:2364 gates the re-anchor on if 4 not in chain_idx — every finger except the one in canonical slot 4 is anchored to its own reachable band; slot 4 keeps a hard-coded thumb_anchor of 1.571 rad. Fine for LEAP, whose thumb really does sit at π/2. Our slot-4 error: perp +0.7°, m05 −48°, baseline +89°, H06_04 +95° — and perp, the only near-correct one, was the only hand that had ever scored.

Two: the range is read at the joint sweep's endpoints and the lookup then interpolates linearly, both of which assume θ(q) is monotonic. It is not for a fingertip passing near the sphere pole, where azimuth wraps and both endpoints land across the branch cut.

Both are fixed, and the fix is verified the strongest way available: regenerating LEAP's own calibration from its URDF and diffing against the shipped artifact leaves slots 0–3 bit-identical, moving only the thumb, by 2.6°. Afterwards every hand has zero pinned slots and slot-to-slot consistency better than LEAP's.

E4d

Why perp holds and never turns

"Never rotates" has two causes needing opposite fixes — the policy is effectively silent on this hand, or the fingers move plenty and the motion does no work on the cube. So we measured the chain end to end: policy action → CIK joint target → delivered joint motion → cube rotation.

The policy is not silent. It emits action magnitudes on our hands within 35 % of what it emits on LEAP. Everything downstream collapses: the joints move 4× less and the cube rotates 4–10× slower.

E6

Authority and conditioning — what actually limits our hands

The type-B command is a pure function of the action, so it can be instrumented offline, with no Isaac and no rollout: reproduce the solve in numpy and sweep the whole action box, seconds per design. The prompt was a plain observation about the renders — on LEAP the cube rests against the phalanges; on ours it rests flat on the palm, so the fingers must travel a long way before they even reach the object.

Two quantities come out, and they are not the same thing. Conditioning: the solve keeps sphere points at distance ≥ l from the joint axis (l = the link length to the next joint) inside a ±0.1 R band, then rotates the link to the extremal survivor. When nothing survives, the target snaps to a joint limit (starved); a target sitting on a limit at all (pinned) carries no information about the sphere. Authority: how far the fingertip travels, in widths of the cube UHAS gives that hand — both the sphere and the cube are sized to the hand, so radians do not compare across designs.

The mechanism is the proximal phalanx as a fraction of reach. UHAS sets R = 0.509 × mean palm-to-fingertip reach, so l/R is scale-free — shrinking the whole hand changes nothing. LEAP spreads ~14 cm of finger over three links (53/36/50 mm) → 0.52. Ours puts 9 cm into one proximal link against a 3 cm distal → 1.3–1.5, two and a half to three times LEAP's. A long first link admits only a thin outer crescent of the sphere, and that crescent empties as the sphere shrinks — which is the starvation.

Status: post-diction on five hands, not validation. The metric has dynamic range where the repose rate has none, and it explains the taxonomy we rendered — but it has never been checked against an outcome, because no design of ours has produced one. It is the filter to spend before Isaac time, to be confirmed by the first design that clears both axes.

E7

The design box, searched — and a clean dominance

E6 said spacing was the lever and the optimum was interior. That was a claim about a landscape nobody had sampled. This is the sample: 980 designs, each a real MJCF → URDF → UHAS-sphere → CIK rebuild — thumb↔pair separation, index↔middle separation, proximal scale and distal scale — about five seconds apiece, no GPU, no Isaac.

Two facts fell out of setting it up. The metric cannot see where the fingers sit on the palm, only how they sit relative to each other: UHAS fits its sphere to the finger roots, so the palm plate never enters, and sliding all three mounts together by 60 mm scores byte-identically. And the cube does not spawn on the palm — init_pos is in sphere-radius units and the sphere is tangent to the fitted palm plane, so every hand gets the cube at 1.3 R off the palm, LEAP and ours alike. Cube-flat-on-the- palm in our renders is a catch failure, not a different spawn.

The perp hand already ships on its collision floor. Its fingers face each other, so the binding contact when the pair closes is index↔middle, not the palm — and it is present at the open pose, so it is resting geometry rather than an artifact of a deep grasp. That makes "shorten the proximal" and "close the fingers" one proposal: the first is the enabler for the second.

The counter-argument, and it has now been run once. authority is fingers-only, so palm contact is invisible to it — and a three-finger hand can use the palm as a fourth contact to gait, which is exactly what a person with three fingers does. Nothing measured above predicts or rules that out, which made it a finetune hypothesis rather than a zero-shot one. E8 ran the finetune. It did not settle the question, and it changed the shape of it.

E8

Both open items, run — and the metric does not survive

E7 left two things outstanding: a finetune, because palm bracing is invisible to a fingers-only kinematic score and so could not be ruled out by one; and a zero-shot spread of built designs, because authority had ranked 976 designs without ever once being checked against an outcome. Both ran. The finetune did not settle its question. The zero-shot answered its own, decisively, against the metric.

The zero-shot check

Six designs, five new plus the shipped perp, each put through the contact solver and each with its cube held to 50–56 mm so that no design can win by shrinking the task — UHAS scales the cube to the hand, so an unconstrained comparison rewards small hands for playing an easier game. Scored on the graded metrics, because the repose rate is floored at 0 % and cannot rank anything.

Two designs were built specifically to test the metric's product form. d4_pin (travel 1.29, 52 % pinned) and d5_lo (travel 0.79, 22 % pinned) reach the same score by opposite routes, and give equal goal approach — 1.552 against 1.547 — with unequal drop, 98.7 against 91.2. The product form is adequate for what the metric measures and irrelevant to what it does not.

The finetune

Four thousand iterations on perp alone, 4096 environments, 82 minutes, resuming the shipped checkpoint. Repose success moved 0.00 % → 0.00 %; closest approach 1.519 → 1.410 rad against a 0.100 rad tolerance. In training, mean episode length climbed from 26 to about 577 steps inside the first hundred iterations and then sat flat for 3 900 more; at evaluation that twentyfold became about fifteen percent, because most of what was learned was coping with the training environment's own randomisation rather than anything the deterministic evaluation applies.

Exploration never collapsed — the action noise stayed flat at 0.53 the whole way — so this is a fair test failing rather than a broken run, and that is the useful part. PPO found a holding basin in under a hundred iterations and never left it. The palm-gaiting hypothesis is therefore not settled: this does not separate “perp cannot gait against the palm” from “PPO cannot find the gait starting from a hold”.

LEAP rolls it on the palm

A different cube face at every sampled step, and the goal marker changes with it. The mechanism is legible: it rests the cube on its palm and rolls it with the fingers.

perp, zero-shot holds, never turns

The cube sits in the palm-side cradle at the base of the fingers, in the same orientation at 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 s.

perp, finetuned indistinguishable

Same cradle, same cube orientation, frozen for the full fifteen seconds. Eighty-two GPU-minutes produced no visible change in deployed behaviour.

d1_hi the best gripper we have built

Centering 0.253 radii, against perp's 0.563 and LEAP's 0.415. The faces are in the identical pose at every sampled step.

One correction falls out of the same work. E7's buildability filter used a separation floor swept at the shipped phalanx lengths, so it only ever saw the floor track the proximal. Re-gating the top forty designs through the contact solver rejects eight of them: a longer distal reaches across the gap the proximal floor was measured to protect. The best collision-free authority is 1.002, not 1.084, so the real headroom over the shipped hand is +8 % rather than +17 %. LEAP is 1.52 either way; the correction only makes the negative stronger.

E4c

A cheap predictor, and an honest null

The checkpoint carries its own observation normalizer — the per-dimension mean and variance over 108 training samples. Comparing a hand's live observations against it scores how far outside the training distribution that hand puts the policy, in about 90 seconds and with no task rollout. If it tracked task success it would be an extremely cheap morphology evaluator.

What this means for morphology optimization

The evaluator exists and runs. Any hand we can export goes from MJCF to a scored Isaac rollout with no GUI step and no per-hand code, and the same frozen weights drive all of them. The zero-variance property we wanted is real and intact.

It has no dynamic range on our hands yet, so it cannot do the job it was wanted for. It discriminates LEAP-from-us, not us-from-us. Until at least one MorphoHand is scoring materially above zero, this is not a design-search evaluator — and using it as one would reproduce, in a new form, exactly the mistake the programme already made once by ranking designs through a noisy estimate.

The most transferable output is the taxonomy, not the rate. "perp has solved the grasp and lacks the manipulation" is a statement about a design that our own reward-trained evaluators never produced in three months, and it came from one render plus one 90-second probe. A grasp-quality metric — perp's centering of 0.560 radii genuinely beats every other hand we have built — is available today from this pipeline and does have dynamic range across our designs, even while the repose rate does not.

What is still open, cheapest first

Revised after E8. Four of the original items are now done: the box is exhausted, the offline metric is retired as a manipulation evaluator, and the finetune reached a holding basin and stopped there.

  1. A gaiting curriculum, or a shaped reward. The one thing the finetune established is that the repose reward is a hard-exploration target from a holding start — the same failure this program already documented in its own RL. A run that is going to test palm bracing has to make rotating rewarded before it is achievable; more iterations of the same objective will not get there. This is the cheapest remaining route to the palm question, which E8 narrowed rather than answered.
  2. The third phalanx. Untouched, and still the only change in evidence that would move manipulation rather than trade it away. Three CIK-solvable joints across four segments against our two across three; no mount position and no phalanx rescale adds a joint.
  3. Promote d1_hi on the grasp track, where the metric is now outcome-validated at −0.94. It beats LEAP on centering, 0.253 against 0.415, and is the best gripper this program has built. It reposes nothing, so it belongs in the grasp lineage and not in the policy lineage.
  4. sphere_factor — a constant in construct_sphere_with_palm, not a property of any hand, never swept: ×0.75 buys +30 % on an unmodified perp. Free, but it shrinks the cube along with the sphere, so it changes the task as well as the representation.
  5. The clamped-anchor variant — anchor the slot-4 finger at the reachable θ closest to the canonical thumb azimuth. Still correct, still one line, still demoted: the E3 anchorfix moved no outcome.

Method

Policy: models/UGAS_Models/All_grippers/model_14999.pt, deterministic, on Isaac Lab 2.1.0 / Isaac Sim 4.5.0. Task: UHAS-Inhand-Repose, 64 environments, 3000 control steps at 60 Hz, evaluation=True, success tolerance 0.1 rad, drop threshold 0.2 m, 30 s episode timer reset on each repose. Intervals are Wilson 95 %. Distances are reported in sphere radii so they are comparable across hand sizes. Renders are 4-environment rollouts under the same policy and checkpoint. Every number here is produced by scripts/uhas_eval.py, uhas_obs_audit.py, uhas_motion_probe.py and uhas_cube_sweep.sh; the running experimental log with the full tables is docs/uhas/CUBE_REPOSE_PLAN.md. Every hand also has a 10-second rollout video under results/uhas/video/ (gitignored, hence easy to miss).

E6 is numpy only, no Isaac: scripts/uhas_cik_authority.py sweeps each slot's two radius actions on a 21×21 grid over 1000 Fibonacci sphere samples, reproducing _pre_physics_step + torch_solve_for_B_joint. Lateral joints are held at q_sphere, which is what the CIK cascade itself uses when advancing through a type-A joint, so that is not an approximation. Each variant is a full rebuild through uhas_build_hand.py → process_urdf.py; the sphere_factor rows use a patched copy of construct_sphere_with_palm. Rows land in docs/experiments/uhas_cik_authority.json.
